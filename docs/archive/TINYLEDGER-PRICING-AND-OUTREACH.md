# TinyLedger — Competitive Pricing & Client Outreach Research
*Prepared 2026-09-16. Business strategy, not legal/financial advice — treat the pricing number as a starting point to test, not a fixed truth.*

## Important context first: TinyLedger is narrower than most of what it's being priced against

TinyLedger is a **billing/accounting tool** — payments, statements, reminders, fee tracking. Most of the big-name "childcare software" below is a full **center-operations platform**: attendance check-in, parent photo/messaging, daily activity reports, staff scheduling, on top of billing. That matters for pricing — you're not just competing dollar-for-dollar with them, you're offering less surface area for a lower price, which is a legitimate and sellable position ("the tool that just does billing, properly, without the R2,000/month tablet-and-messaging suite"), not a weakness to apologize for.

## What competitors actually charge

| Product | Scope | Price |
|---|---|---|
| **Koonection** (South Africa) | Preschool/crèche management, similar local market to you | **R499/month flat** (~$27), no per-learner or per-teacher fees, no setup fee |
| **Brightwheel** (US, category leader) | Full center management (billing + attendance + parent messaging + photos) | Base $15–30/month **plus** $2–3 per enrolled child/month. For a 25-child class: roughly $65–105/month |
| **Lillio** (formerly HiMama, US) | Full center management | ~$8–12 **per child**/month. For 25 children: **$200–300/month** ($2,400–3,600/year) |
| **Sage Business Cloud Accounting** (general small-business accounting, not preschool-specific) | Billing/accounting only — closest scope match to TinyLedger, just not built for your niche | $20/month (Start) → $40/month (Standard) → $50/month (Plus) |

Two things stand out:

1. **Your current $50/month is priced like the full-featured global category leaders, not like a billing-only tool.** Sage — a general accounting tool with a much bigger company behind it — starts at $20/month for exactly the scope TinyLedger covers (before add-ons).
2. **Your direct local competitor, Koonection, is R499/month (~$27) flat, no per-child fees, and appears to do more than TinyLedger currently does** (full crèche management, not just billing). If a South African preschool owner compares your $50/month billing-only tool to their R499 all-in-one tool, you lose that comparison on price *and* scope today.

## Realistic pricing recommendation

A flat, no-per-child-fee price around **$25–30/month** (or the rand equivalent, since Paystack settles in ZAR anyway) is a defensible, realistic minimum that:

- Undercuts Sage's own billing-only starting tier
- Sits close to Koonection's real local price, so you're not the expensive outlier in your own backyard
- Is dramatically cheaper than Brightwheel or Lillio for anyone comparing globally, which is a strong selling point ("no per-child fees — pay the same whether you have 10 kids or 100")
- Still clears your entire ~$26/month infrastructure cost with a single paying customer (per the launch budget), so it's not a break-even-only price — there's real margin per client from day one

You can keep $50/month (or higher) as a real target for later, once you have case studies, a parent-communication feature, or are selling to bigger centers that can absorb it — but as a *first* price to actually get people saying yes, $25–30/month flat is the more realistic number given what's actually being charged around you right now. This is a starting point to test with real conversations, not a number to treat as fixed — if prospects say yes immediately with no hesitation, that's usually a signal you could charge more.

## Client outreach — which channel actually fits your situation

You asked: ads, organic social, or cold email? Here's the honest breakdown for a solo, bootstrapped founder in a small, well-defined local niche (preschool/crèche owners):

**Cold outreach — the best starting channel for you specifically, and it's legitimate, not "scraping."** Preschools publish their own contact/inquiry emails on their own websites and Google Business listings precisely so people can reach out — that's a public business directory, not a privacy violation, as long as you're emailing a business inquiry address about a business tool (not a personal address, and you include an easy way to opt out). The current research on cold email backs this up directly: small, targeted lists dramatically outperform bulk sends — campaigns under 50 contacts get a **5.8% reply rate vs. 2.1% for mass blasts**. Given you likely know your city/region's preschools by name already, a personalized, small-batch approach (10–20 emails a week, each one referencing that specific school) will outperform anything automated at volume. Paid ads, by contrast, cost real money before you've validated the pitch works at all — a LinkedIn ad averages roughly $700 in cost per customer acquired, and search ads aren't far behind — money you don't have to spend yet when a personal email costs nothing but time.

**Organic content — a slow-building complement, not a starting point.** Posting consistently (school-owner-focused tips, "how we help preschools track fees" content) builds trust and inbound interest over months, but it needs sustained, repeated effort before it produces real leads — not something that gets you your first 5–10 clients quickly on its own.

**Referrals — worth building in from client #1, likely your best long-term channel.** Preschool owners in the same city or province tend to know each other (associations, WhatsApp groups, shared suppliers). Once you have even 2–3 happy paying schools, asking them directly for an introduction to another owner they know, or offering something like a free month for a successful referral, tends to convert far better than any cold channel — because it arrives with built-in trust.

**Paid ads — hold off until you have proof it works.** Not because ads don't work, but because they're the most expensive way to learn whether your pitch and price actually land. Validate the offer with free, targeted cold outreach first; once you know what messaging gets a "yes," ads become a way to scale something proven rather than a way to discover it.

### Suggested order to actually get your first clients

1. List 20–30 real preschools in your area/country with a published contact email.
2. Send short, personalized emails (not a mail-merge blast) — mention the specific school by name, the one problem TinyLedger solves (tracking who's paid, who owes what), and offer a free trial period.
3. Ask every "yes" and every existing contact for one introduction to another preschool owner they know.
4. Once you have a few paying schools and can point to a real result ("saved X hours a month," "collected R-amount faster"), start light organic posting using that as proof.
5. Only look at paid ads once cold outreach + referrals have validated the price and pitch — use ad spend to scale a message you already know converts, not to find one.
